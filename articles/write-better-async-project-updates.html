How to write better async project updates

Good written updates help people understand what changed and what needs attention.

NEXORA EDITORIAL · UPDATED NOVEMBER 23, 2026 · BETTER WORKFLOWS

Write for the reader’s next decision

An update is useful when it helps someone understand progress or decide what to do. Start with the current status and the most important change since the last update. Avoid a diary of every action if those details do not affect the project.

Put urgent blockers and decisions near the top. Readers should not have to scan a long paragraph to discover that work is waiting for approval.

Separate completed work from planned work

State what is finished, what is in progress, and what comes next. These categories prevent a planned task from sounding like a completed deliverable. Link to the relevant file or ticket when the reader needs evidence or context.

Use concrete descriptions. “Made progress on design” is less useful than “Completed the mobile layout draft; desktop review remains.”

Describe blockers with a clear request

A blocker should explain what is preventing progress, what information or decision is needed, who can provide it, and when it becomes important. “Waiting on feedback” can be improved by naming the specific review and the affected milestone.

If there is no immediate action required, say so. This prevents every update from creating unnecessary urgency.

Make risks visible early

A risk is something that may happen; a blocker is something already preventing progress. Distinguish the two and explain the possible impact. Early warning gives people more options than a last-minute surprise.

Do not hide uncertainty behind confident language. State what is known, what is assumed, and when the situation will be checked again.

Choose a predictable rhythm

Agree on where updates live and how often they are expected. A regular rhythm reduces repeated status requests, but it should match the project’s pace. Small tasks may need fewer updates than complex work with many dependencies.

Use meetings for discussion that benefits from live interaction. Routine progress, links, and decisions can often be recorded asynchronously so people in different schedules can respond thoughtfully.

Record decisions where the work lives

When a decision changes scope, timing, or priorities, record it in the project’s shared source of truth. A chat message can alert people, but important decisions should remain findable after the conversation moves on.

Keep sensitive personal or client information out of broad status channels. Share only the details necessary for the people who need to act.

A reusable update format

A good update is brief but complete. If a reader needs more detail, link to the underlying task or document instead of copying every project detail into the message.

A practical template

Use this outline as a starting point and adapt it to the project. Keep assumptions visible and remove fields that do not apply.

STATUS: [on track / needs attention / blocked]
SINCE LAST UPDATE: [completed work]
IN PROGRESS: [current task]
NEXT: [next milestone]
BLOCKER: [what is needed, from whom]
RISK: [possible impact]
DECISION NEEDED: [question and deadline]
LINKS: [source of truth]

Nexora note: this guide is educational and provides general information. Adapt the suggestions to your circumstances and check applicable professional, legal, security, or accessibility requirements where relevant.
